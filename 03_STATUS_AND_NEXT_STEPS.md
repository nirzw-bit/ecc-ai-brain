## Ordered next steps
1. **Secure the source.** Obtain a durable, authorized copy from the owner using a private transfer route. Do not rely on this session’s temporary preview path. Preserve the original read-only and record filename, byte length, and SHA-256 at receipt.
2. **Resolve permission before analysis beyond necessary private review.** Confirm what the recorder is authorized to do with the recording and whether the unaware participant can be approached safely and appropriately. Separate permission for private analysis, transcription, AI processing, quotation, voice use, and public release. Do not assume one permission covers all.
3. **Set an approved processing boundary.** Prefer local processing if practical. Before any third-party tool, verify current cost, retention, training, access, and deletion terms, then obtain authorization consistent with that boundary.
4. **Assess language and audio quality.** With authorized access, listen privately and document language(s), intelligibility, noise, and overlap. Use Speaker A/B only; do not identify participants.
5. **Create an auditable transcript.** Timestamp utterances; preserve original wording; mark uncertain words, overlap, and unintelligible spans; keep translation distinct from the original; have a human verify machine suggestions against the audio.
6. **Build an idea inventory from verified speech.** Attribute ideas cautiously, separate explicit statements from interpretation, and list open questions rather than filling gaps.
7. **Research only the validated questions.** Verify genuinely free tools and relevant legal/privacy issues from authoritative current sources. Do not make jurisdiction-specific claims without location/context.
8. **Reassess release options.** Seek informed review and permission for any public use. If permission is not available, do not publish identifiable voice or material; consider a newly narrated, anonymized account only after a separate harm and accuracy review and clear labeling.

## Exit criteria for Phase 1
A verified transcript or documented reason it cannot be produced; language and speaker attribution confidence; a source-grounded idea inventory; a privacy/consent decision record; and explicit separation of facts, uncertain interpretations, and unresolved questions. A transcript alone does not clear publication.
